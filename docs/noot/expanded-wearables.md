# Expanded Noot wearables

Authored and exported through Blender MCP using `scripts/blender/noot_fashion.py` and `noot_accessories.py`. The editable library is `assets/noot/fashion.blend`; the existing Noot Studio scene is preserved. Inventory thumbnails are rendered from those meshes by `noot_wearable_previews.py`.

The wardrobe offers 12 head choices, 9 eye choices, 9 foot choices and 14 clothing choices, including the original/bare options. Added cap, beret, visor, crown, party hat, flower crown; square, cat-eye, aviator, heart, star and sport frames; loafers, sandals, slippers and ballet flats. Round frames and all existing shoes were rebuilt. Hats clear the note stem; eyewear has curved lenses, frames, bridges, hinges and temples. Shoes use separate soles, toe domes, ankle openings and style-specific closures. Closed shoe uppers cover the original toes; sandals expose them intentionally.

Headwear and frames bind rigidly to the head, shoes to the left/right foot bones. Every player owns their materials and skeleton while sharing immutable decoded geometry. Models download by selected item, with a shared promise/cache; the character no longer waits for the full wardrobe. A late download respects the current selection, wakes paused/reduced-motion scenes, and cannot remount a disposed character. The full library would have cost about 1.38 MB gzip; individual shipped item chunks range from about 13 KB for sandals to 103 KB for the raincoat. No CPU vertex uploads are needed during motion.

Frame color is independent from hat, shoe, cloth and trim colors. The shared appearance parser validates the new IDs and colors for saved profiles and multiplayer serialization. Existing choices retain their defaults.

Dress and ballet hems use a damped spring driven by pose rhythm and motion impulses, with a pinned waist and neckline. Torso/forearm clearance accounts for the wider skirt envelope. This is bounded secondary animation and approximate collision clearance, not a general cloth simulation. Reduced motion resets the hem; pause freezes it; long frame gaps are clamped.

Validation: 161 tests passed, including toe coverage, bone attachment, distinct geometry, stale selections, load/disposal races, independent colors, persistence, shared buffers and cloth impulse/settling behavior. Six fully dressed players were reviewed in the shared WebGL stage; this machine showed 8.3 ms median / 9.3 ms p95 frame intervals and 2.1 ms p95 CPU work per frame (201 scene draw calls, 474,444 scene triangles at pixel ratio 1.25). This is a local sample, not a mobile hardware guarantee. The heaviest tested complete wardrobe remains below 25,000 accessory triangles and 13 accessory draw calls per character. Production build and targeted lint pass. Mobile inventory scrolling and thumbnails were reviewed at 390 × 844.
